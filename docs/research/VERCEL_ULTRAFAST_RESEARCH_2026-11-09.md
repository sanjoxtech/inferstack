# Vercel's 1,000+ tokens/s announcement: verification and InferStack implications

Researched 9 November 2026. This is public-source research plus one unauthenticated metadata observation, not an inference benchmark. No paid inference calls, credentials, cluster changes or serving-code edits were made. Concurrent implementation changes in the workspace were preserved.

## Finding

The news is real. Vercel's 7 November announcement adds DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 Ultrafast to AI Gateway, with a claim of generation above 1,000 tokens/s, a 1M context window and public-preview availability. It serves the October 17 checkpoint through Fireworks' accelerated endpoint. Vercel is the gateway/integration layer; Fireworks supplies the accelerated inference. This is not evidence that Vercel built a new open-source 1,000-tokens/s engine. [Original announcement](https://vercel.com/changelog/deepseek-v4-1-flash-1017-ultrafast-now-available-on-ai-gateway).

The specific news is about Fireworks, not Cerebras. High-speed Cerebras integrations are a separate serving route and cannot explain this announcement.

## What I observed from the public endpoint

At 03:52:53 UTC / 09:22:53 IST on 9 November, I fetched the public model-endpoints metadata. The response identifies Fireworks as the provider and exposes the following rolling one-hour aggregates:

| Measurement | P50 | P95 |
|---|---:|---:|
| Reported output throughput | 893 tokens/s | 1,347.65 tokens/s |
| Reported time to first token | 659 ms | 1,568.45 ms |

These are Vercel's reported aggregates of customer traffic, not measurements generated by this review. Sample counts, prompt/output distributions, cache conditions, reasoning mix and concurrency are not disclosed in this response. They support the narrower proposition that some requests exceed 1,000 tokens/s; they do not establish that every workload or the median request exceeds it. P95 throughput is the fast end of a throughput distribution; slow-tail throughput requires a lower percentile. The throughput and latency percentiles are marginal summaries and must not be combined into a purported representative request.

The response also advertises a 1,048,576-token context, tool use, structured outputs, image input, prefix caching, and provider data-policy flags. These are metadata claims, not capabilities or retention guarantees independently tested here. Context capacity does not imply that a million-token cold prompt runs at the headline generation speed.

## Speed has an explicit price

Fireworks' current official pricing distinguishes the standard checkpoint from its Ultrafast serving route:

| Route | Input USD / 1M | Cached input USD / 1M | Output USD / 1M |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 standard | 0.30 | 0.006 | 1.20 |
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash 1017 Ultra-fast Preview | 2.40 | 0.048 | 9.60 |

All three listed rates are 8x the standard route. This is a premium serving mode, not a free throughput improvement. Fireworks' documentation targets 6–8x generation speed, with larger gains claimed for some coding tasks; those are vendor targets, not results independently reproduced here. Its direct identifier is accounts/fireworks/routers/deepseek-v4p1-flash-1017-ultrafast. [Serverless modes](https://docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/serverless-modes), [current price table](https://docs.fireworks.ai/serverless/pricing).

Vercel's corresponding slug is deepseek/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017-ultrafast. Its page lists the same uncached-input/output rates. The cache price is rounded on the web page; the public JSON records 0.048 USD/M precisely. [Vercel model page](https://vercel.com/ai-gateway/models/deepseek-v4.1-flash-1017-ultrafast).

Illustrative arithmetic, not a workload quote: 10,000 uncached input tokens plus 2,000 output tokens cost $0.0054 on standard and $0.0432 on Ultrafast at these rates, before any unaccounted separately billable usage. The premium is $0.0378 per such request. Reasoning tokens, retries, actual output length and cache reuse must be counted under the provider's billing rules.

## Why this model can be fast, and what remains an inference

DeepSeek describes an asymmetric MoE with 552B backbone parameters, 8B active for input and 16B active for output, and substantially reduced KV memory relative to V4 Flash. Those are model-architecture properties. [DeepSeek announcement](https://www.deepseek.com/en/news/deepseek-v4-1-flash/).

The public vLLM implementation adds concrete execution options: DSpark drafts several tokens per round for target verification, while its recipe configures device-dependent verification and parallelism. That makes model-specific speculation a supported research direction. It does not prove Fireworks uses the exact same algorithm or flags. Acceptance rate, draft cost and workload concurrency can change whether speculation helps at all. Adaptive or approximate verification also needs its own numerical/quality qualification; a generic claim of lossless speculative sampling cannot be inherited by every optimization variant.

Capacity is substantial. The public vLLM recipe describes about 510.3 GB of checkpoint tensors and a default variant with a 614 GB VRAM minimum; its smallest listed NVIDIA profile for that layout uses 8x80 GB. These are recipe-specific constraints, not universal minima across quantization/offload configurations. A single Colab A100 can test smaller supported models and the technique framework, but cannot reproduce this full checkpoint/profile. Active parameter count is not the model's total resident memory requirement. [Pinned recipe](https://github.com/vllm-project/vllm/blob/199c2e4bff8df75c2d927e4dc5fe6880e4055109/recipes/models/deepseek-ai/DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash.yaml).

Do not conflate four different claims:

- Per-request generation speed after first content.
- Full request latency including queue, prefill, network and decode.
- Aggregate output tokens/s across concurrent requests and devices.
- Completed, correct agent tasks per dollar and per unit time.

Reasoning and visible output must also be accounted for separately where the provider exposes them. A faster token stream can still produce a slower or more expensive task if it reasons longer, repeats tools or fails validation.

## Recommended addendum to InferStack's implementation work

Keep the P0–P5 cloud-serving plan. Add two bounded research tracks rather than replacing the serving architecture with Vercel:

### A. External accelerated-route qualification

Register standard and Ultrafast as separate capability-limited provider profiles for the same named checkpoint. Compare direct Fireworks standard, direct Fireworks Ultrafast and Vercel Ultrafast where credentials/capacity are available. Vercel is optional for unified billing/routing; direct Fireworks is the useful comparison for isolating gateway overhead. These routes can serve as optimizer models or external serving endpoints, but those roles remain separate contracts.

Pin requested checkpoint, provider, tier, reasoning effort, generation settings, cache policy and workload hash. Disable fallback/model substitution during trials and record the actual route on every result. Mark remote weights, kernels, hardware and runtime identity as unknown unless independently observable. Such an endpoint cannot inherit self-hosted runtime-attestation claims just from its slug.

Acceptance: streaming content, cancellation, tool calls, structured outputs and usage accounting work on held-out tasks; failures/partial outputs never pass; quality remains within budget; measured p95 latency and cost per correct completed task justify the premium. Add an optional bounded provider qualification gate, not a hard dependency for all deployments.

### B. Open-source speculation experiment

Register engine-supported speculative decoding as a versioned technique, including target and draft artifact identities, algorithm, draft length, verification mode and supported hardware. Begin with a compatible model that fits available hardware; the full V4.1 profile is a separate multi-GPU qualification.

Run the same target with speculation off and on; use batch/concurrency-dependent draft policy only after the static baseline is measured. Record accepted/proposed tokens, verification time, draft time, peak memory, client TTFT, engine decode metrics, total goodput and quality. Rejected draft tokens are not useful output tokens. Do not infer acceptance from SSE chunk count. Numerical/token-distribution gates and task-level quality gates apply to each verification configuration.

Acceptance: a repeated improvement in the chosen latency or SLO-qualified capacity objective, no unbudgeted quality regression, and safe restart/rollback of the changed serving profile. Do not promise 1,000 tokens/s as a generic hardware-independent target.

### Reproducible comparison protocol

Predeclare a token/spend/time cap before any paid calls. Use the held-out coding/tool/JSON/reasoning workload with fixed task success tests. Include cold and warm prefixes, short/medium/long prompts within each endpoint's limits, natural and controlled output lengths, and explicit reasoning settings. Sweep concurrency and offered load separately; avoid a closed-loop client silently hiding queue pressure. Record actual send timing and client saturation.

Use independent repeats and a frozen sample-adequacy policy for p95/p99; no significance claim from a few demo requests. Report errors, cancellations, truncation and incomplete runs, alongside raw timing and authoritative usage. Benchmark the model/route changes with fixed infrastructure first, then evaluate routing and autoscaling separately. Preserve trusted evaluator isolation, frozen identities and complete cleanup from P0–P5.

## Decision

Treat Fireworks Ultrafast as a credible premium route to benchmark and public engine-supported speculative decoding as a high-value experiment. The news strengthens the case for InferStack's measured speed/quality/cost comparison and workload-aware optimization. It does not eliminate weight management, routing, autoscaling, rollout safety or live GPU qualification, and it does not provide a freely reproducible 1,000-tokens/s serving stack.
